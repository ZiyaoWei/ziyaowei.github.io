Theremin

Like a real theremin, one hand plays pitch and the other plays volume: the strip on the left is volume, the rest of the pad is pitch, higher to the right. On a touchscreen, put a finger down on each and slide. With a mouse or trackpad, just hover — there's nothing to press — and scroll or two-finger swipe to change the volume.

 

Bring the volume down, lift your finger or move off the pad to fall silent. Turn off Separate volume to play with one finger: left–right is pitch and up–down is volume. Like a real theremin, nothing is marked — turn on Show notes to see where the notes are. The pitch field is Linear, with every octave the same width, as on a modern theremin like the Moog Etherwave; Uncorrected is the raw physics without that correction, where each octave is half as wide as the one below and the high notes crowd together. Tone follows how a theremin makes its sound: two radio oscillators beat against each other and a detector picks out the beat — Classic is a clean detector, Bright one that only catches the peaks, and Pure sine an ideal one. Trackpads drive the cursor, so they always play a single voice — multitouch chords need a touchscreen.

Pick a piece under Play along and its notes fall toward the line; the music only moves while you're on the right note, and through a rest only while you're silent (lift your finger or bring the volume down). Near the ends of each note there's a moment of grace for sliding between notes, as a theremin does, so take your time. The piano plays Rachmaninoff's accompaniment and moves with you, waiting when you wait. The dashed line on the volume strip follows the score's dynamics; it's only a suggestion and never holds the music up.
